# Adding your own party

The parties are data, not code: `config/parties/fictional.yaml` lists them, and every scenario
includes that file. A new party takes part in every election created after you add it — the
world clock creates elections when it reaches their date ([CLOCK.md](CLOCK.md)), so it shows up
in the next ones, and in the whole history after `python -m app demo --force`.

## Step by step

### 1. Define the party

Add an entry at the end of `config/parties/fictional.yaml`, indented like the others:

```yaml
  - code: KP                      # 2–12 capital letters/digits, starting with a letter
    name: Koffiepartij
    abbreviation: KP
    color: "#8E5B3A"
    family: centrist / local
    description: My own fictional party of coffee lovers.
    ideology: {economic: 0.1, social: -0.2, europe: 0.3}   # each −1 … +1
    base_share: 0.06              # long-run national share
    turnout_propensity: 0.0       # + = its voters turn out more
    founded_year: 2023
    demographics: {pct_age_15_25: 0.15, pct_education_high: 0.08}
    urbanity: {"1": 0.10, "5": -0.10}        # 1 = very urban … 5 = rural
    provinces: {NB: 0.30}                    # extra strength per province
    regions: {university_towns: 0.20}        # named regions of config/regions.yaml
    municipalities: {GM0855: 0.40}           # Tilburg
```

- **Ideology** places the party on three axes: economic (−1 left … +1 right), social
  (−1 progressive … +1 conservative), Europe (−1 eurosceptic … +1 pro-European). Voters of other
  parties drift to parties close to them, and nonpartisan candidates are judged on it too.
- **Where it is strong**: `demographics` (utility per standard deviation of a REAL CBS indicator:
  `log_density, urbanity, pct_age_15_25, pct_age_25_45, pct_age_65_plus,
  pct_households_with_children, pct_single_households, pct_origin_europe, pct_origin_non_europe,
  pct_education_high, pct_education_low, income_per_capita_keur, pct_owner_occupied`),
  `urbanity`, `provinces`, `regions` (bible_belt, catholic_south, randstad_core,
  university_towns, twente, … — see `config/regions.yaml`) and `municipalities` (CBS codes).
  Values are shifts on a logit scale: 0.3 is a clear boost, 1.0 a very strong one.
- To find a municipality's CBS code:
  ```bash
  python -c "import pandas as pd; m=pd.read_parquet('data/processed/geo_2025/municipalities.parquet'); print(m[m.name.str.contains('Tilburg')][['code','name']])"
  ```

### 2. Give it votes in the built-in scenarios

`config/scenarios/midterm_2026.yaml` and `config/scenarios/general_2028.yaml` fix every party's
national share in `calibration: national:`. Add your party there and lower the others so the
total stays at 1.0:

```yaml
calibration:
  national: {PA: 0.24, VLP: 0.23, NVB: 0.12, CVU: 0.11, DM: 0.07, SAP: 0.06, PLB: 0.07, RV: 0.03, KP: 0.07}
```

A party missing from that list gets only about 1 %. The 2024 scenario has no such line and uses
`base_share`. Elections after 2028 are generated by the world clock: their national mood starts
from the previous result and drifts back towards `base_share`, so a new party grows towards its
`base_share` over a few elections.

### 3. Decide where it runs candidates (optional)

By default a party runs a candidate wherever its expected share is at least 6 % (House, Senate,
governors) or 10 % (mayors). Change that per scenario under `house:`, `senate:`, `governors:` and
`municipal:` → `contest_rules:`:

```yaml
    KP: {always: true}                                    # everywhere
    KP: {min_expected_share: 0.03, provinces: [NB]}       # or: only in Noord-Brabant, from 3 %
```

School boards and water boards are nonpartisan; the party shapes their candidates' positions
automatically. Municipal councils (party lists) include every party.

### 4. Run for president (optional)

In `founding_2024.yaml` and `general_2028.yaml`, add two people under `candidates:` (copy an
existing entry and change `key`, the names, `party: KP` and `home_municipality`), then a ticket
under `president: tickets:`:

```yaml
    - {party: KP, president: anna-bakker, vice_president: tom-jansen}
```

From 2032 on, the world clock gives every party with at least 10 % nationally a ticket of its own
(led by its governors, senators or House members, or by new people).

### 5. Check, then rebuild

```bash
python -m app scenario show demo-2028          # your party is listed with its share
python -m app scenario validate demo-2028      # also run it for midterm-2026 and founding-2024
python -m app demo --force                     # rebuild the world with the new party
```

`config/polling.yaml` holds pollster biases per party; a new party simply gets none. Its colour
can also be overridden per user in the app (Settings).
